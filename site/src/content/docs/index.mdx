<Section eyebrow="The authoring contract">

## You write the left. Onebox owns the right.

A Compose file you wrote cannot be the contract — it describes containers, not intent. Turn fields on and watch what gets derived.

<Derivation>

- `shop-web-1` — the container, with a one-based replica ordinal that is never omitted

- `shop_default` — the application's own Compose network

- `onebox-proxy` — Traefik, its static configuration, a router and TLS

- `/var/lib/ob/shop` — releases, current, journal and services

</Derivation>

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="How it works">

## Four commands, two artifacts.

Nothing reaches the host without both of them, and both expire. Any drift or local change requires a new plan.

<Lifecycle>

1. `ob validate` — **Declare**

   Read the project file, resolve every shorthand, contact nothing.

2. `ob plan` — **Observe and propose**

   Inspect the host, compare it to the declaration, and seal the result: the typed operation graph, exact config, host state, image digests and rendered Compose.

3. `ob approve` — **Confirm, locally**

   A tamper-evident confirmation covering that plan, target, risk and expiry. Migrations use the strong ceremony, where the operator types the release id.

4. `ob deploy` — **Execute under a lock**

   Health-gated rollout, traffic drain, verification, then a versioned release with an explicit predecessor.

</Lifecycle>

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="What you get">

## The whole operational surface.

Not a roadmap — everything here runs today. [Shipped vs proposed](/status/capabilities) is the full account.

<Features>

- **Plan before apply**

  A sealed, digest-bound plan you read before anything runs.

- **Zero-downtime rolls**

  Health-gated rolling replacement, with traffic drained first.

- **Rollback**

  Every release has an explicit predecessor to go back to.

- **Resume and abort**

  An interrupted deploy leaves a checkpoint, not wreckage.

- **Managed databases**

  Eleven drivers, each with a volume, a health check and a credential.

- **PostgreSQL backups**

  Continuous archiving, point-in-time restore, and a restore drill.

- **SOPS secrets**

  The whole declared graph rotates as one opaque generation.

- **Locks and fencing**

  One writer at a time, and an invalid epoch fails closed.

- **Generated Compose**

  Print it with `ob preview`, keep it for good with `ob eject`.

- **Proxy and TLS**

  Traefik, routers and certificates — or bring your own proxy.

- **Typed output**

  One envelope, JSON or NDJSON, for every finite machine result.

- **Scheduled jobs**

  Cron whose meaning is preserved exactly, or refused at load.

</Features>

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="What it refuses">

## Refusing is the feature.

A tool that guesses produces a container that starts and stores nothing durable, or a job that runs at nearly the right time.

<Refusals>

- `at load`

  **A roll with nothing to gate on**

  `strategy: rolling` with no health check. A rolling workload cannot publish a host port either, because two replicas cannot hold the same port during a roll.

- `at load`

  **A cron whose meaning would change**

  A schedule Onebox cannot preserve exactly, rather than one approximated. A job that runs at nearly the right time is a job that ran at the wrong time.

- `at load`

  **A driver it cannot run**

  Eleven drivers are supported. A twelfth name is refused, because inventing an image from it produces a container that starts and stores nothing durable.

- `shorthand_and_workloads`

  **Two ways of saying the same thing**

  Top-level workload shorthand mixed with a `workloads` block. It would be ambiguous which workload the top-level fields describe, so neither reading is chosen.

- `host_owner_mismatch`

  **A second application on a claimed host**

  Ownership is recorded at bootstrap and checked by every mutating command. It is released only once nothing of the application remains.

- `at plan`

  **A promise it cannot keep**

  A backup policy on a driver that cannot honour one, and a major version change a driver cannot perform in place — rather than replacing the container and leaving the data intact and unreachable.

</Refusals>

</Section>

<Section eyebrow="Limits">

## What it does not do today.

Published here rather than three clicks away, because a capability list that omits its gaps is how an operator ends up trusting a backup that was never taken.

<Rows>

- **Backups cover managed services, not workload volumes**

  A service declaring `backup` — PostgreSQL today — is archived off-host and restorable to a point in time. A workload's own volume is not, and `ob doctor` says so for every workload holding durable data.

- **`mongodb` runs a standalone server, not a replica set**

  An application needing change streams or multi-document transactions will connect, authenticate, and then fail — in its own logs, not in anything Onebox says.

- **One host, and no failover**

  Rolling deployment can avoid interruption while the box is healthy. It cannot make a failed physical host available. Recovery onto another host is a separate, evidence-backed workflow.

[Shipped vs proposed](/status/capabilities) is the full account: what the binary does today, and what the schema accepts but cannot yet execute.

</Rows>

</Section>
